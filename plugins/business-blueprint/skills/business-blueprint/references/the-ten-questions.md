# The ten questions, and what a real answer looks like

Read this in Stage 1. The questions are deliberately plain. The work is not in the
wording, it is in refusing to accept a category where a person belongs.

Two profiles answer them. **A business owner** has customers, a price and a shop or a
service. **A salaried person** plans a side project or a change inside their own work;
for them the customer may be a manager, the price may be hours saved, and the capacity
is the evening. If their message does not say which, ask in the same message as the ten,
and read each question through that lens.
The example lines are shapes of a strong answer. The agent never fills one in.

The person answers from memory. Guessing is allowed as long as it is called a guess.
"I do not know" keeps the question open and is a better answer than an invention.

---

**1. What am I trying to change?**

Strong: one sentence about a change in the world, not a product name. Business: one
thing that goes differently in the shop next month. Job: one task at work that takes less
time, or one side client served.
Weak: "an app", "a website", "a platform", "learn AI". Those are shapes, not outcomes.
Follow up only if the answer is a tool with no outcome: "and when it works, what is
different for someone?"

**2. Who is the first real person this is for?**

The most important question on the list, and the one most often answered wrong.
Strong: one trade, one area, one size, one habit you have seen, so specific that you
could phone one of them this week. Job: your manager, one colleague, one team,
or one person who would pay you on the side. A salaried person often says "my company";
a company cannot be phoned either. Ask who in it.
Weak: "small businesses", "students", "everyone", "SMEs in India". A category cannot be
phoned, and a plan aimed at a category is a plan aimed at nobody.
This is the one follow-up always worth asking: "name one person who fits, or describe one
closely enough that I would recognise them in a room." If they cannot, that is not a
failure. It is the biggest [PENDING] in the plan, and it belongs in question 10.

**3. Where do I meet them today, or where would I find them?**

Strong: a place, a group, a shop, a search someone types, a person who introduces them.
Job: the office, the team chat, a weekly meeting, one person who introduces you.
Weak: "social media", "online", "marketing". Those are budgets, not places.

**4. What goes wrong for them on a normal day, that I have seen?**

Strong: something observable that happens on a normal day, before this business exists,
with a count if they have one: a thing lost, a thing redone, a thing waited for. Job:
the thing a manager or colleague redoes, waits for, or complains about.
Weak: the absence of the product ("they do not have a website", "they do not use AI"). Nobody wakes up missing
a website. A problem the customer would not name themselves is an Assumption; label it.

**5. What will they get from me, in one sentence they would say themselves?**

Strong: what someone gets, in words they would use, small enough to deliver next month.
Job: the thing you hand your boss, or the result a side client gets.
Weak: a feature list, a platform with three sides, or "help them with AI".

**6. What is it worth, and to whom?**

Strong: a number, even a rough one, and where it came from ("what she pays the boy who
answers the phone"). For a salaried person the number is rarely a price: hours saved a
week, or the raise, the role or the side income it leads to. "Not sure yet" is a real
answer here and stays [PENDING] until a real person gives a number; it is the question
most people with a job get stuck on, so say so.
Weak: "affordable", "premium", "we will figure out pricing later". Price is a claim about
who the customer is. A plan with no number has not chosen a customer.
Never supply a number for them. A price you invent becomes a price they defend.

**7. How much can I take on?**

Strong: customers a week with today's people, hours a week, skills present and absent,
what already exists. Job: hours a week outside work, and the limits the job sets: company
data, tools and clients you may not touch. Those limits are facts, write them down.
Weak: "full time". Ask what else is in the week.

**8. What can I put in, and for how long?**

Strong: a number and a period, and what happens when it runs out. Hours a week, rupees a
month (an AI subscription counts), and how many months before they expect something back.
Weak: silence. Keep it [PENDING] rather than assuming zero or assuming plenty.

**9. What have I already seen or heard that supports this?**

Strong: anything real. One conversation, one sale, one message from a stranger, one
count from a spreadsheet. Say who said it and when.
Weak: "I know this market", "everyone I speak to loves it". Experience is not evidence,
and friends are not a sample. Both get labelled Assumption without argument.
This question decides what the attack can score highly. Most plans score low here, and
that is the normal, honest starting point, not an insult.

**10. What is my biggest unknown or decision, the one that, if wrong, makes the rest pointless?**

Strong: the thing that, answered wrong, makes everything else pointless. Business:
usually a question about the customer or the money. Job: usually whether this lives
inside the job or outside it, or whether anyone would pay.
Weak: a small worry, or a tool choice. If their answer is a tool, ask again: "if that
tool was solved tomorrow, would the business be certain?"
If they say "nothing", the plan has an invisible hole. Say so once, plainly, and put the
weakest of the previous nine answers here as [PENDING].

---

## Writing them into v0

Keep their words. Keep names, numbers, places and prices exactly as given, including
awkward phrasing. A tidied sentence loses the customer's own language, and that language
is what the survey and the landing page later need.

Mark each line: fact, Assumption, or [PENDING]. When an answer contains both, split it.
"Salon owners, they all struggle with bookings" is one fact and one Assumption.

Unanswered questions are not gaps in the file. They are the file doing its job.

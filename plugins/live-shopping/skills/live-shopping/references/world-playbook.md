# The world playbook, for a seller in India

What live selling in China and TV selling in the United States teach, one principle at a time, and
what each one looks like on Instagram or Facebook in India. Where a principle comes from
a named practice, the source is listed at the bottom. Where it is this kit's own advice,
it says so. Nothing here is a promise of sales.

## 1. A schedule, like a programme

In China it is common for brands to stream weekly, or even daily (MIT Sloan Management
Review).

**In India, this kit's advice:** one show, same day, same time, every week, so buyers know
when to come back. Put the day and time in the bio,
in every post about the show, and in the WhatsApp broadcast. Change the time only after
the scorecard shows the buyers are elsewhere.

## 2. One featured offer, real and time-bound

QVC, the American TV shopping channel founded in 1986, has a featured offer it calls
Today's Special Value (Wikipedia, QVC).

**In India, this kit's advice:** at most one show-only price per show, on one product. Say
the price, say exactly when it ends ("until [exact time] tonight"), and end it at the time
you said. A late buyer pays the normal price; say so kindly. A countdown that resets is
not an offer; see `honest-urgency.md`.

## 3. Every product has a job in the running order

One Chinese operator guide (人人都是产品经理, woshipm.com) sorts a live room's products
into five types, and calls three of them the most important:

- **引流款, the traffic item:** holds viewers in the room and lifts the number watching.
  Wide appeal, strong value, low price, sometimes sold at a loss. It goes first.
- **爆品, the bestseller:** the product proven to sell.
- **利润款, the profit item:** high margin, brought out at the room's peak.
- The other two: **品牌款, the brand item,** which lifts how the room is valued, and
  **常规款, regular items,** tested to find the next bestseller.

The guide says a room cannot do without the traffic item and the bestseller, and that a
seller working alone may keep just those two: the traffic item draws the crowd, the
bestseller earns.

**In India:** this kit calls them the opener, the bestseller, the profit items and an
optional anchor, and adds a closer of its own, so a first show has a reason to stay to
the end. Two rules the Chinese guide does not need to say, because the Indian rules do:
the anchor is a real product at its real price, and a traffic item sold cheaply is sold
cheaply for real, in the quantity said.

## 4. Show what a photo cannot (this kit's advice)

A live beats a catalogue only when it does something a picture cannot: the saree draped
and walked in, the cake cut to show the layers, the oil poured to show how thin it is,
the bag packed to show how much it holds. If a segment could be replaced by a photo and a
price, it is not using the live.

**In India:** hold it close to the camera, in daylight or under a white light, and do the
thing. Then answer the question the comments ask, out loud, by name.

## 5. Rehearse in public before you are live (this kit's advice)

A seller who cannot go live yet, or has never gone live, films each product segment on
its own as a short video, as it will be said live: who it is for, show it, prove it, the
price, how to buy, but without the count or any show-only price (see
`segment-script.md`, "Recorded, not live"). Each one ends with the show's day and time
once the Live screen says yes, and until then with an invitation to follow.

**In India:** post them as Reels and Facebook videos in the weeks before the first show.
Every post rehearses the host, tests which product people respond to, and announces the
show. For a seller below the follower line, this can help reach it with real followers,
not bought ones. It is not a promise.

## 6. Two people: the host and the order desk (this kit's advice)

The host never stops to type. A second person, on a second phone, watches the comments,
messages every buyer on WhatsApp, confirms payment, and tells the host the real count.

**In India:** the second person is usually family or one helper. One person can run a
first show alone if they pause for 60 seconds after each product to note the codes, and
message every buyer within the hour after the show.

## 7. Your own list, after the show

Chinese sellers call it private traffic: turning an occasional shopper into a follower
through personal chats on WeChat, instead of meeting them only on an open marketplace
(Rest of World).

**In India:** the list is on WhatsApp. After a buyer pays, invite them, with their yes,
to a broadcast list, a Channel or a Community, ask them to save the business number, and
tell them the next show there first. The list is the one audience the seller holds
directly.

## 8. Cut the show into short videos (this kit's advice)

The best 30 to 60 seconds of each product segment becomes a Reel the next day, with the
count and any show-only price cut out, because they were true only during the live. The
live is watched once; the clips can keep working after it, and they announce the next
show.

## 9. Review every show with your own numbers (this kit's advice)

After each show, five numbers into the scorecard: peak viewers, comments, orders paid,
money received, and returns a week later. One change for the next show. The seller's own
shows are the benchmark. Industry averages from other countries, other categories and
other platforms say nothing about this seller.

## What not to copy

- **Hours on the first day.** Some Chinese sellers stream for hours. A first show is 30 to
  45 minutes.
- **The famous host.** Paying a big host to sell is a big-brand model. The seller is the
  host; their own face and voice are the trust.
- **The fake shopper.** The Rest of World article on private traffic describes sales staff
  posing as interested consumers. On WhatsApp the seller is always the seller, by name.
- **Shouting and fake scarcity.** A seller in India who says there are fewer pieces than
  there are, or that a product is more popular than it is, is using a dark pattern the
  consumer regulator lists by name. Real counts only; see `honest-urgency.md`.

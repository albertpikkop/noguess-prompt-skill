# Honest urgency: what a live seller may say

India's Central Consumer Protection Authority issued the Guidelines for Prevention and
Regulation of Dark Patterns on 30 November 2023, listing 13 dark patterns (Government of
India press release; the text is on the authority's guidelines page). Trilegal, a law
firm, summarises them as covering platforms, advertisers and sellers, meaning any person
who sells or markets a product in the course of business, which this kit reads as
including a seller running a live sale. Seven of the 13 are easy to fall into on a live. Each one below: what it
looks like on a live, what to say instead, and what to keep.

The rule lines below follow Trilegal's summary. The guidelines say their examples are
guidance and each case is decided on its own. This page is not legal advice; for their
own case, a seller asks a lawyer.

In the "say instead" lines, words in [brackets] are where the seller's own numbers go.
Never fill them with a guess.

## 1. False urgency

**The rule:** creating or implying a false sense of urgency or scarcity to push a
purchase. Saying stock is more limited than it is, or a product is more popular than it
is. Trilegal reads the guidelines as putting the burden on the seller: if questioned, the
seller has to show the claim was a correct representation.

**On a live it looks like:** "Only 2 left!" when there are 50. A timer that resets. "200
people are buying this right now" when nobody counted. The same "last piece" held up
three times.

**Say instead:** the real count, from the order desk, updated after each paid order: "Is design ke [total] piece the. [paid] ka payment aa
gaya, [left] bache hain." A show-only price with its real end time, ended when it ends.

**Keep:** the order desk's count sheet from every show. It is the record that every
scarcity line was true.

## 2. Drip pricing

**The rule:** not showing the full price up front; parts of the price revealed later.

**On a live it looks like:** "Sirf 999!" on the live, then 150 for delivery in the
WhatsApp message.

**Say instead:** the price and the delivery charge together, in the same breath, every
time: "[price], plus [delivery charge] delivery." The WhatsApp message repeats the same
two numbers.

## 3. Bait and switch

**The rule:** advertising one thing and, when the buyer is ready to pay, saying it is gone
and offering something similar but more expensive.

**On a live it looks like:** the cheap opener "sells out" the moment the buyer messages,
and the desk offers the pricier piece instead.

**Say instead:** if a product sells out, say so on air with the real count. The desk may
offer an alternative only if the buyer asks, and never as if it were the same product at
the same price.

## 4. Nagging

**The rule:** disrupting or annoying a buyer without their permission, with repeated
requests, to make a sale.

**On a live it looks like:** five WhatsApp messages to someone who commented a code and
did not pay.

**Say instead:** one message with the details, and one reminder at most. After that,
silence. This kit's advice: a buyer who wants it will write back.

## 5. Disguised advertisement

**The rule:** presenting paid or planted content as if it were a real user's review or
opinion. On a platform, disclosing it is the seller's or advertiser's job.

**On a live it looks like:** a friend in the comments pretending to be a happy customer. A
paid creator reading praise without saying they are paid.

**Say instead:** real customers' words, read only with their permission. A guest who is
paid or given the product says so on air.

## 6. Confirm shaming

**The rule:** using fear, shame or guilt to push a purchase.

**On a live it looks like:** "Sirf smart log hi ye deal lenge." "You will regret it
tonight."

**Say instead:** who the product is for, and who it is not for. Saying "this is not for
you if you want [X]" is honest, and it keeps the order desk free for real buyers.

## 7. Basket sneaking

**The rule:** adding items or charges the buyer did not agree to. Delivery charges and
taxes that are clearly disclosed are not sneaking.

**On a live it looks like:** a gift box, a "packing charge" or an add-on quietly included
in the total sent on WhatsApp.

**Say instead:** only what the buyer chose, and the delivery charge that was said on air.
Anything extra is offered as a question, never added.

## Also worth knowing

The Advertising Standards Council of India published its own guidelines on deceptive
design patterns in advertising in June 2023. The direction is the same: say what is true,
show all of the price, never pressure with a lie.

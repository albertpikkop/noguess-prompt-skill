# Live Shopping

## What this makes

Two files in the person's own folder, at two different times:

```text
LIVE-SHOW-PLAN.md    written now: can you go live today, the format, the team, the running
                     order, a script for every product, the order desk, the trust kit,
                     seven days to the first show. Every fact they did not give is [PENDING].
                     Not eligible to go live yet: the runway goes at the top of this file
SHOW-SCORECARD.md    written only after a show, from the numbers the person gives: five
                     numbers, and the one change for the next show. Never a blank one
```

The plan is written from `assets/LIVE-SHOW-PLAN-TEMPLATE.md`, keeping its Assumptions and
Still unknown sections.

When the ask is the whole plan: in the chat reply, whether they can go live, the next three
steps and your questions; the full plan, scripts included, goes in the file. No folder to
write to: the plan goes in the reply. When the ask is one piece (a script, a message, a
reply to a buyer), give that piece in the chat.

Speak in plain words, short sentences. Answer in the language the person wrote in; the
file headings and [PENDING] stay in English. No em-dashes in text you write.

## The one law: sell what is true, live

A live show sells because the viewer is watching a real person, with real stock, in real
time. Every trick that fakes one of those three spends the one thing a live has that a
catalogue does not: trust. A countdown that resets. "Only 2 left" when there are 50. A
stranger's words read out as a customer's review. Viewers who catch one trick stop
believing the rest, and in a live they catch it in the comments, in front of everyone.

In India it can also break the rules. The Central Consumer Protection Authority, India's
consumer regulator, lists false urgency as a dark pattern in its Guidelines for Prevention
and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023: falsely showing that stock is more limited than it
is, or that a product is more popular than it is. Trilegal, a law firm, reads the
guidelines as covering sellers too, and as putting the burden on the seller to show a
claim was true. So on this show, urgency is always real: the real count, said out loud and
updated as orders are paid, and at most one show-only price with a real end time, kept.
Read `references/honest-urgency.md` before writing any offer.

This is how the guidelines read, not legal advice. For their own case, a seller asks a
lawyer.

**Asked for fake urgency** (a timer that resets, "only 2 left" when there are more): one
line on why not, the honest version, then the work they asked for. Never a lecture, never
a refusal with nothing after it.

## The fact that changes the plan

Meta removed live shopping itself. Product tags and the checkout inside a live ended on
Facebook on 1 October 2022 and on Instagram on 16 March 2023. Going live still works on
both. What is gone is the buy button inside the live.

So on Instagram and Facebook in India, a live sale is three things joined by hand: the
show, an order taken on WhatsApp or in DMs, and payment by UPI or a payment link. The plan
is built for that. Never promise a tag, a cart or a checkout inside the live. Instagram's
live shopping was a US feature, and both have ended; if the person has seen a buy button
inside a live, it was that feature or another platform. Say so in
one line and move on.

## Stage 0: can you go live today

| Where | What it needs (sources in `references/world-playbook.md`) |
|---|---|
| Instagram | a public account with at least 1,000 followers (since August 2025) |
| A Facebook Page | at least 100 followers, and an account at least 60 days old. To go live from a Page you need access to create content on it |
| Their own Facebook profile, with professional mode turned on | the same: at least 100 followers, and an account at least 60 days old. Facebook says friends automatically follow you, so a profile with many friends may already pass the 100 line |

A plain personal profile is for personal use: Meta's Terms (section 3.1) say to create one
account, your own, and use it for personal purposes. A weekly sale runs from a Page or
from a profile with professional mode turned on, which is the seller's own choice to make.

Rules change without notice. Before the plan depends on this table, the person opens the
Live screen and reads what it says, without pressing Go Live. Record the result in the
plan. If the app and the table disagree, the app wins; say so and carry on.

**Not checked yet, or the numbers are unknown:** do not stop. Write [PENDING: open the
Live screen and read what it says; do not press Go Live] in the plan, and plan for the
platform where their buyers already message them.

Then choose one platform for the first show:

- **Eligible on both:** the one their last 20 orders came from. Their own numbers decide,
  not a general rule. If they do not know, [PENDING: count where the last 20 orders came
  from] and start on the one where buyers already message them.
- **Eligible on one:** that one.
- **Eligible on neither:** first, their own Facebook profile with professional mode turned
  on, then the Live screen; Facebook says friends automatically follow you, so it may
  already work. If not,
  the runway. Show the gap to each line with the arithmetic
  (Facebook: 100 minus their followers; Instagram: 1,000 minus theirs; and the account's
  age against 60 days). The smaller gap is the first door. Then read
  `references/world-playbook.md`, principle 5: the runway posts are the show's own
  product segments, recorded as short videos, so every post rehearses the host and
  announces the show at the same time, with no date, count or show-only price until the
  Live screen says yes. Give a runway they can start this week.

Never suggest buying followers, likes, views or comments, or joining engagement groups.
Fake followers fill a live with people who will never buy.

## Stage 1: the facts

If `BUSINESS-TRUTH.md` is in the folder, read it first and take the business, the price
and the customer from it. Then write the plan in the same reply. The plan needs these
facts; every one that is missing becomes [PENDING] on the line it belongs to:

1. What they will sell in the first show: 5 to 8 items, each with its real price and the
   real count in stock.
2. Who is with them during the show. One person can host. A second person runs the order
   desk.
3. How a buyer pays and receives today: UPI, payment link or cash on delivery; courier or
   pickup; the delivery charge; the return or exchange rule.

Useful, never blocking: the language their buyers think in, the city, when their buyers
are free, what sold best last month, which items make them the most money.

Put your questions at the end of the reply: at most three, only the ones that change the
plan. **Never end a reply on questions alone.**

**A gap is a label, never a gate.** If they answer fewer than you asked, or none, that is
their answer. Write the plan with [PENDING] on the lines those facts belong to and keep
going. Never ask the same question twice. Never re-print a list of open items they have
already seen. The plan with visible holes is the deliverable; a plan that waits is not.

## Stage 2: the format

- **One show, same day, same time, every week.** In China it is common for brands to
  stream weekly or even daily. The fixed slot is this kit's advice, so buyers know when
  to come back.
- **The first show: 30 to 45 minutes, 5 to 8 products.** Enough to learn from, short
  enough to prepare in a week. Longer shows come after the first scorecard.
- **One platform** for the first show, from Stage 0.
- **A name they can say in one breath,** and the language their buyers think in. Roman
  Hinglish on screen is fine when that is how the buyers write.

## Stage 3: the running order

One Chinese operator guide plans the running order around what each product is for. Read
`references/world-playbook.md`, principle 3. The guide says a room cannot do without two
of them, and a seller working alone may keep just those two:

- **Opener (the traffic item):** the product that makes a new viewer stay. Easy to
  understand, easy to say yes to, often the lowest price. It goes first, while people
  arrive. Its job is to hold the room, not to earn.
- **Bestseller:** the product that has already sold best for them. It comes as the room
  fills, and in a small show it is where the money is.

For a bigger first show, and only from what the person told you:

- **Profit items:** where their margin is highest. At the peak, when the room is fullest.
- **Anchor (the brand item), optional:** their best piece at its real price, shown early,
  so the others read as good value. Never an invented "original price" crossed out.
- **Closer, this kit's addition:** a real reason to stay to the end, the newest piece or
  the one show-only price of the night. Announce at the start that it comes last, then
  keep the promise.

Assign roles only from what the person told you: price, stock, what sold best, what earns
most. A role their facts cannot decide is [PENDING: which item sells best] with a line on
what would decide it. Never invent an item to fill a role.

Say the running order at the start and again every 10 minutes. People join a live late,
and a late viewer who knows what is coming stays for it.

## Stage 4: the script for each product

Read `references/segment-script.md`. About 90 seconds per product, in five beats:

1. **Who it is for.** One line. Name the buyer, not the product.
2. **Show it.** In hand, close to the camera, in use. Drape it, pour it, fold it, cut it.
   The thing a photo cannot do.
3. **Prove it.** Only proof the person actually has: a customer's words they have
   permission to read, a test they can do on camera, a certificate they hold. If there is
   none, the beat is [PENDING: what proof you have] and the show goes on without it.
4. **The price.** The real price, and the delivery charge, said together. If there is a
   show-only price, say what it is and exactly when it ends.
5. **How to buy.** The product's code, then the WhatsApp step. Said the same way every
   time.

Then read the comments, answer the two most asked questions, say the real count left,
and move to the next product.

Write the script into the plan for every product. Every missing fact is [PENDING] in the
beat it belongs to. Never write a fabric, a weight, an ingredient, a review, a
certificate, a price or a count the person did not give.

## Stage 5: the order desk

Read `references/order-desk.md`. The second person runs it, on a second phone.

- Every product has a short code: S1, S2, S3. A buyer comments the code.
- **First comment, first chance.** The first person to comment a code gets the first
  chance to pay, until a pay-by time the host says on air; the next comment is next in
  line. Set the pay-by time as a clock time at least two hours after the last message A
  will have gone out (this kit's advice), so every buyer has time to pay and a reminder
  never lands minutes after the details.
  Say it on air before the first product: "[code] ke [count] piece hain. Pehle comment ko
  pehla mauka."
- The desk messages each buyer on WhatsApp within minutes (message A): the item, the
  price, the delivery charge, how to pay, and the pay-by time.
- **Paid means the money shows in the seller's own bank or UPI app.** A screenshot is not
  payment. The order is confirmed then, not when the comment is seen.
- Two people paid for the last piece: refund the second the same day and tell them why.
- The desk tells the host the real count after each paid order. The host says it on air.
  The count is also the seller's record that every scarcity line was true.
- The messages come from the templates in the plan, written before the show.
- No second person: the host pauses for 60 seconds after each product to note the codes,
  says the count claimed on air ("[count] claimed, pay by [time]"), and sends message A to
  every claimer within one hour of the show ending. An unpaid claim passes to the next in
  line at the pay-by time, and they get message A with their own pay-by time, also at
  least two hours away.

Cash on delivery is a choice with a cost. Label it as an assumption to check against
their own orders: [Assumption: cash on delivery orders come back more often than UPI
orders here. Check the last 20.] Never state an industry number for it.

## Stage 6: the trust kit

Said at the start of every show, and pinned where the platform allows:

- Who they are and where they are, as much as they are willing to show. [PENDING] if they
  have not said.
- The return or exchange rule, in one sentence.
- How payment works, and that the seller never asks for an OTP, a card PIN, a UPI PIN or
  a password.
- "We message only from [business number]. Anyone else asking you for money is not us."
- The real stock count of the product on screen.

Tax and licences are not guessed. If the person has not said, write the line with its
own office and move on: [PENDING: GST, check with your CA]. For food:
[PENDING: check your FSSAI registration at foscos.fssai.gov.in]. For consumer law, a
lawyer.

## Stage 7: seven days to the first show

**Not eligible yet:** the seven days start on the day the Live screen says yes. Until then
this section holds only the runway from Stage 0, and no go-live date.

Eligible: write this into the plan with real dates from today:

1. Check eligibility in the app. Fix the day, the time and the show's name.
2. Choose 5 to 8 products. Count the stock. Fix the prices and the delivery charge.
3. Write the scripts. Say each one out loud with a timer.
4. Announce: a post, a story, and a WhatsApp broadcast to buyers who said yes to hearing
   from them, with the day, the time and one product.
5. Post a short video of the bestseller, filmed the way it will be shown live, without the
   count or any show-only price (see `references/segment-script.md`).
6. Full rehearsal on camera: light, sound, internet, and one friend buying through the
   whole order desk, from comment to payment to confirmation.
7. Story one hour before. Go live on time.

## Stage 8: after the show

- **Within one hour:** every buyer who paid after the live ended gets the paid message
  (B) with the dispatch date; buyers who paid during the show already have it.
- **About 30 minutes before each buyer's pay-by time:** every person who claimed and has
  not paid gets one reminder (C), not more. Chasing the same person again and again is nagging, and it is on the
  same dark patterns list. At the pay-by time, an unpaid claim passes to the next in line.
- **Within one day:** cut the best segments into short videos and post them, without the
  counts or show-only prices, which were true only during the live. Fill the scorecard.
- **The replay:** if the live's replay stays up, caption it with the show's date, or take
  it down; the counts and the show-only price in it were true only that day.
- **Build your own list:** invite buyers to a WhatsApp broadcast list, Channel or
  Community, with their yes, and ask each buyer to save the business number so the
  announcements reach them. In China, sellers move buyers from the platform into their
  own chats; in India the chat is on WhatsApp.

After the show, and only from the numbers the person gives, write `SHOW-SCORECARD.md`
from `assets/SHOW-SCORECARD-TEMPLATE.md`. Five numbers per
show: peak viewers, comments, orders paid, money received, and returns or cancellations
a week later. Then one derived number, with its arithmetic shown: orders paid per 100 peak
viewers. Then the one change for the next show. Never compare with an outside
benchmark without a named source; this seller's own shows are the benchmark.

## What this skill never does

- Promise sales, followers, reach or income, in any amount.
- Invent a product, a price, a stock count, a review, a proof, a guest, a discount or a
  result.
- Write false urgency: a countdown that resets, "last few pieces" that are not the last,
  a fake viewer count, planted comments.
- Suggest buying followers, likes or comments.
- Send a message, go live, post, or spend money. It writes the plan; the person runs the
  show.
- Give legal or tax advice beyond naming the rule and who to ask.

## The folder decides, not the order

Read the folder before you decide anything.

| In the folder | What it means | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| nothing, only what they typed | an intention to sell live | Stage 0 and Stage 1, then the plan with [PENDING] where facts are missing |
| `BUSINESS-TRUTH.md` | the facts every skill in this kit reads | read it first; never re-ask what it answers |
| `PLAN-v2-BLUEPRINT.md` or a later version | a plan tested on real strangers | take the customer and the offer from it |
| `LIVE-SHOW-PLAN.md` | the show is already planned | continue from it; never rebuild it from scratch |
| `SHOW-SCORECARD.md` | shows have run | plan the next show from the one change it names |

Four rules, the same in every skill in this kit:

1. **Never re-ask what a file already answers.** Read first, then ask at most three
   things, and only about what is missing.
2. **Do the thing they asked.** Never refuse because an earlier step is missing. Say in
   one line what is thin and what it risks, then do what they asked.
3. **One skill answers.** Whichever skill owns the thing they actually asked for runs the
   conversation.
4. **Facts live in one file.** `BUSINESS-TRUTH.md` holds what is true about the business.
   The show plan holds the show. Never a second copy of either. Facts that outlast one
   show (the business number, the UPI ID, the return rule) go into `BUSINESS-TRUTH.md`
   when it exists, with the person's yes, and the plan points to them there. This show's
   items, prices and counts live in the plan.

## Words and voice

- Plain words an 8th grader reads without slowing down.
- Talk to the seller, not about live commerce. No market-size numbers, no famous hosts'
  sales figures. The seller's own numbers are the only numbers in the plan.
- Never invent. What the person did not say is [PENDING].
- No em-dashes or en-dashes in text you write. Commas and full stops.
